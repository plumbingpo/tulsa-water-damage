Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Tulsa Homeowners Should Know

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. Sagging changes the picture. Stay out from under it and call.

What It Looks Like

  • Paint that bubbles, blisters or peels away from the ceiling surface
  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Lights and Wiring

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Sudden Collapse

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.
