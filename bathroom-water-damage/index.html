Bathroom Water Damage: What Tulsa Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Tulsa homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
