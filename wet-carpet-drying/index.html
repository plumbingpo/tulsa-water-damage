Wet Carpet Drying in Tulsa

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Tulsa extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Signs to Look For

  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip

What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Subfloor Damage

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.
