How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Tulsa?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Tulsa?

Typical Cost at a Glance

Typical Tulsa water damage projects cost from $1,500 up to $10,000 or more. With clean water and a small area, the total can stay below about $1,500. Rebuilding after a sewage backup or a serious flood can push the cost far past $10,000.

Those numbers describe what is typical nationally; they are not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in Tulsa, OK move the numbers up or down. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

Pricing by Category of Water

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. After about two days of sitting, water that started clean carries bacteria and is reclassified to gray or black. Putting things off raises the price for this reason, among others.

The Main Price Factors

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. Here is what a contractor looks at when pricing a job.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Handling is least expensive when the water is clean. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Given time, water works its way into wall cavities and subfloors. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are often billed by the day. If your house is dry after three days, you pay less than if it needs six.

Cost by Common Job

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in Tulsa, national figures usually look like this. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Drying and cleanup frequently total $1,500 to $5,000, and the burst pipe repair is on top of that. A pipe that ran for hours, or wet more than one level, costs more.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Is Water Damage in Tulsa Covered by Insurance?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

Coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. For sewer backup, a dedicated add-on is often required.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. Your deductible also matters. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Ways to Lower the Cost

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Within 24 to 48 hours, mold growth can get underway. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. If you can do it safely, lift boxes, furniture and rugs clear of the wet floor.

If the power has not been cut, never wade into standing water, and avoid any water that could carry sewage. Saving money is never worth an infection or an electric shock. Water that entered from outdoors calls for professional flood cleanup by people wearing the correct protection.

How to Get an Exact Price

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. An inspection with a moisture meter shows how far the water travelled and what it will take to dry and repair.

If you need water damage restoration in Tulsa, OK, the best time to ask for that inspection is now, while the damage is still small.
