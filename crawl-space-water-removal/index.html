Crawl Space Water Removal: What Tulsa Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.
